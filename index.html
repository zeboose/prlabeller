Why this exists

If you print labels on a LabelWriter, the software you use is probably an Intel application, and Apple is retiring Intel support.

VersionWhat happens to an Intel app
macOS 26.4 Every launch warns that it will stop working. Repeatedly.
macOS 27 Rosetta is no longer installed. The app runs only after you reinstall Rosetta on demand — which, when your labelling software suddenly refuses to open, looks exactly like it is broken.
macOS 28 It stops.

The printer was never the problem. The LabelWriter driver has been universal for years. Only the application was Intel-only. So the sensible answer is to replace the application, not the printer.

And upgrading DYMO's own software does not solve it. Neither DYMO Label v8 nor the newer DYMO Connect has an Apple Silicon build — both are Intel-only, and both depend on Rosetta. The advice you will see in most forum threads, "install DYMO Connect instead", moves the problem to the same cliff rather than avoiding it.

PR Labeller — "Post Rosetta" — is that replacement. It reads and writes the same files, and prints through the same driver.

What it does

  • Opens your existing labels and renders them faithfully. Every one of the 133 label files used to develop it renders pixel-identically to the reference, and saving one back produces a byte-identical file. Your templates are working assets; software that quietly reformats them is not tidying up, it is losing your work.
  • Prints to the printer and stock your own driver reports, one label or a run of them.
  • Fills in addresses from an existing address book (.abx) or a CSV, one contact or a list.
  • Edits and creates — text, addresses, pictures, barcodes and shapes, with alignment, snapping, layering and unlimited undo.
  • Rotates freely, at any angle rather than only right angles.
  • Prints straight. An alignment test and sub-millimetre offsets, because every machine grips the stock slightly differently and no amount of software can guess by how much.

Questions people are asking

My DYMO label software has stopped working on my Mac. What happened?

Almost certainly Rosetta. DYMO's label software is an Intel application, and Apple is retiring the translation layer that lets Intel software run on Apple Silicon. macOS 27 uninstalls Rosetta during the upgrade, so an app that worked last week refuses this week. Nothing is wrong with your printer and nothing is wrong with your Mac.

Is there an Apple Silicon version of DYMO Label?

No. There is no Apple Silicon build of DYMO Label v8, and none of DYMO Connect either. Both are Intel-only.

Will installing DYMO Connect fix it?

No — that is the most common piece of advice on this subject and it does not work, because Connect is Intel-only too. It will run today under Rosetta and stop at the same point v8 does.

Do I have to buy a new label printer?

No. Your LabelWriter is fine. Its driver has been universal — native on Apple Silicon — for years. Only the application was stuck, which is what this replaces.

Will it work on my M1, M2, M3 or M4 Mac?

Yes, natively, with no Rosetta involved. It also runs natively on Intel Macs; the download is a universal build.

Will it open the labels I already have?

Yes, if they are .label files — DYMO Label v8's format. It opens and saves them without altering them: of the 133 label files used to develop it, every one renders pixel-identically to its reference and saves back byte-identical. Files created by DYMO Connect are a different format and are not supported.

Can I still use my address book?

Yes. It reads DYMO address books (.abx) and plain CSV files, prints one label or a run of them, and lets you edit contacts with dated backups taken before every change.

Do I have to buy special labels?

No. PR Labeller changes nothing about your stock. It asks your own printer driver what sizes it takes and offers you exactly those.

Requirements

  • macOS 14 or later, on Apple Silicon or Intel
  • A LabelWriter with its driver installed — the same driver you use now
Drag the app to your Applications folder before opening it. An app run straight from a mounted disk image is put in a read-only holding area by macOS, where it cannot remember your printer, your folders or your window size.

What this is not

PR Labeller is an independent program. It is not made by, endorsed by, or connected to Sanford, L.P. or its subsidiaries. DYMO is their trademark, used here only to say plainly which files this program reads and which printers it prints to.

It contains none of their software — no templates, no stock catalogue, no driver files. Every label size it offers is read from the driver already installed on your own Mac, every time it runs.

It is not on the App Store. The App Store requires the sandbox, and the sandbox forbids both reading the printer driver's own configuration and the print path that was proved correct against real hardware. So it is distributed directly instead — signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarised by Apple, which is what lets it open in one step rather than behind a warning about unverified software.

On label rolls. Some newer LabelWriter models accept only rolls the printer itself recognises. That is decided inside the printer's own firmware, before this program is involved at all.

PR Labeller does nothing about it, and never will. Not as an option, not as a plug-in, not on request. Please do not ask.

Something wrong?

In the app: Settings ▸ Diagnostics ▸ Send…, or Help ▸ Report a Problem. It opens a message with a technical summary already in it — versions, your printer and its driver, the label sizes the driver offered, and the alignment in use.

It contains no addresses, no contacts and nothing from your labels, and nothing is sent by the app: you read it, delete anything you would rather not send, and press send yourself. Say what you were doing, what you expected, and what happened instead — that sentence is usually worth more than everything underneath it.
